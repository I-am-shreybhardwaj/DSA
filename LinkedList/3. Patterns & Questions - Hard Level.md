## Learning Roadmap

1. Start with **medium LinkedList patterns** if not already mastered.
2. Focus on **advanced two pointers and fast/slow pointer** in complex scenarios.
3. Attempt **reverse k-group and complex reversals**.
4. Tackle **cycle detection variants and analysis**.
5. Master **sorting and merging optimization** (merge sort for LinkedLists).
6. Solve **copy with random pointer and LRU/LFU cache**.
7. Explore **flattening and complex restructuring**.
8. Consolidate with **hard classic problems** for interviews and competitive programming.

## Advanced Techniques

- **Dummy Nodes**: Essential for clean edge case handling.
- **Floyd's Algorithm**: Master cycle detection in O(1) space.
- **Merge Sort**: Best sorting for LinkedLists (O(n log n) time, O(log n) space).
- **Two Pass Approach**: Often used for finding middle, length, etc.
- **Recursion Carefully**: Stack depth is a concern; prefer iterative where possible.
- **Fast & Slow Pointer**: Powerful for detecting cycles, finding middle, and complex traversals.
- **Hash Maps**: Useful for copy operations and caching.
- **Reverse & Reuse**: Often more efficient than creating new structures.

---

## Key Takeaways

- LinkedList problems often require **multiple passes** or **clever pointer manipulation**.
- **Space optimization** is crucial - prefer O(1) space when possible.
- **Dummy nodes** simplify edge cases dramatically.
- **Floyd's cycle detection** is essential for hard problems.
- **Merge sort** is the go-to sorting algorithm for LinkedLists.
- Practice both **iterative and recursive** approaches for comparison.
